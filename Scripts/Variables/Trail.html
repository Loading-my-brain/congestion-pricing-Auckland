3. SPATIAL ROUTING PARAMETERS AND JUSTIFICATION

Transit travel times and network distances are calculated using the r5r (Rapid Realistic Routing) package, which compiles the Auckland street network (OpenStreetMap) and transit schedules (GTFS) into a single routing graph. The following parameters are implemented and justified based on academic literature and local planning guidelines:

Cumulative Walking Limit (30 Minutes)

  • Parameter: max_walk_time = 30
  • Justification: A typical multimodal transit journey is composed of multiple walking legs, including the initial access walk to the stop, transfer walks between different services, and the final egress walk from the CBD transit stop to the workplace.
  • Auckland-Specific Standards: Local bus stop catchments are planned at 400m to 800m (representing a 5-to-10-minute walk) according to Auckland Transport’s Transport Design Manual (2020). Rapid rail and ferry catchments are significantly larger, with users accepting walking distances of 800m to 1,500m (10 to 20 minutes) based on Auckland Council’s RIMU (2013) Walkable Catchments report.
  • The 30-Minute Budget: A 30-minute cumulative limit represents the logical sum of these standard Auckland transfer legs (e.g., a 10-minute access walk, a 5-minute transfer walk, and a 15-minute egress walk). Cumulative walking durations beyond 30 minutes (equivalent to more than 2.4 km of walking at a standard 4.8 km/h pace) exceed standard physical commuting tolerances, prompting travelers to transition entirely to private vehicles (Alshalalfah & Shalaby, 2007; El-Geneidy et al., 2014).

Maximum Trip Duration (120 Minutes)

  • Parameter: max_trip_duration = 120
  • Justification: Auckland is a geographically vast, monocentric region with outer-peripheral commuter belts (such as Pukekohe and Helensville) under Auckland Council’s boundary. A peak-hour train or bus journey from these outer limits to the CBD often requires 80 to 105 minutes.
  • Preventing Data Truncation: A 120-minute (2-hour) cap is required to capture the full regional commuter footprint without artificially deleting those outer-suburban, transit-reliant communities who face the highest transport equity penalties. Commutes exceeding 2 hours represent extreme outliers outside standard daily travel-time budgets (Marchetti, 1994).

4. TEMPORAL PARAMETERS AND JUSTIFICATION

  • Parameter: departure_datetime = Tuesday, 12 May 2026, 8:00 AM
  • Justification 1 (GTFS Calendar Window): Based on the GTFS timetable metadata, the active schedule window runs from May 1, 2026, to July 31, 2026, with some services not starting until May 9, 2026. Selecting May 12 guarantees that 100% of Auckland Transport’s active winter schedules are represented in the model.
  • Justification 2 (Typical Weekday Baseline): Tuesdays and Wednesdays are the standard days used by transport geographers to model peak travel behavior. They avoid the atypical, flexible travel behaviors associated with Mondays and Fridays (which are heavily skewed by hybrid work-from-home or early weekend travel).
  • Justification 3 (School Term Week): In New Zealand, Term 2 of the 2026 school year officially runs from Monday, 20 April to Friday, 3 July 2026. Selecting May 12 ensures the model runs during a standard school term week, avoiding school holiday periods when Auckland Transport reduces its bus frequencies.
  • Justification 4 (No Holiday Disruptions): May 12, 2026, is completely free of any public holidays or regional anniversaries (such as King’s Birthday or Easter), ensuring the routing engine does not run on a restricted weekend-style timetable.

5. BILATERAL ORIGIN-DESTINATION (OD) FLOW ANALYSIS

To transition the analysis from static neighborhood profiles to dynamic spatial flows, the 2023 Census “Journey to Work” (JTW) dataset is integrated.

  • The Dataset: This dataset contains 19,334 Auckland-to-Auckland commute links, connecting each Origin SA2 (usual residence) to each Destination SA2 (workplace).
  • Desire Lines (Commuter Flows): Because each row represents a flow, we convert these links into spatial “desire lines” (LINESTRING geometries) connecting the centroids of the origin and destination SA2s. This allows us to map the physical corridors of Auckland’s commutes.
  • Testing Spatial Mismatch: By calculating the physical length of these lines in kilometers (st_length), we can run a weighted correlation against our origin income profiles. This allows us to test the Spatial Mismatch Hypothesis, evaluating whether lower-income Aucklanders are forced to travel significantly longer physical distances to reach their jobs compared to wealthier cohorts.
